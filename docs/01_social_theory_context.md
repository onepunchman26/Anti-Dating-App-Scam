# Social Theory Context

This project begins from the idea that dating-app scams are not only a fraud problem. They are also a trust-infrastructure problem.

## Social Capital Decline

Social capital describes the networks, norms, and mutual expectations that make cooperation easier. When social capital declines, people may have fewer trusted intermediaries who can help them interpret character, reputation, and risk.

AI-SlowMatch does not try to recreate traditional authority. It explores whether a technical system can support cautious, user-controlled trust building without becoming a surveillance layer.

## Loss Of Relational Intermediaries

Traditional intermediaries included family introductions, friend networks, local community reputation, shared institutions, and repeated offline interaction. Their decline means more people enter intimate contexts as strangers with little shared accountability.

## Platform Capitalism

Platform capitalism turns social interaction into mediated activity that can be ranked, monetized, and optimized. In dating, this can mean attention loops, premium visibility, paid filters, and growth incentives that do not always align with user safety or long-term trust.

## Surveillance Capitalism

Surveillance capitalism describes business models that extract behavioral data for prediction and influence. AI-SlowMatch must avoid becoming a deeper surveillance layer. It should minimize data, avoid hidden scraping, and make retention and deletion explicit.

## Online Dating User Experience Split

Online dating can create sharply different experiences. Some users face overwhelming attention and safety concerns. Others face invisibility, rejection, or paid anxiety. Scammers exploit both conditions: fear, loneliness, urgency, and the desire for certainty.

## Fertility And Marriage Decline As Social Symptom

Fertility and marriage decline should be treated as social symptoms, not moral panic. They may reflect economic pressure, gender distrust, individual preference, delayed adulthood, housing costs, weaker institutions, and changing norms. This project does not prescribe marriage or commitment. It supports safer trust formation for users who choose to seek relationships.
